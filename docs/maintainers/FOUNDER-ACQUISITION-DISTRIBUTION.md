## The additional company problem

A good product may never reach a large number of paying software businesses. The original founder-growth plan describes how Relay discovers a commercial opportunity, executes an approved website edit, verifies the result, and learns from the evidence. The $100M ARR ambition also requires a **repeatable customer acquisition motion for Relay itself**, strong activation, renewal, and expansion into larger customer budgets.

More outbound messages, free SEO users, installations, GitHub stars, generated articles or page impressions do not substitute for paying customers who return for useful work. A founder-level product can become a meaningful business with low-touch distribution, but only if the cost of finding, onboarding, supporting and retaining a customer stays small relative to actual gross-profit contribution.

This document makes Relay's distribution hypothesis inspectable, starting from its existing public SEO research software and the [Relay SEO Check GitHub Action](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay-seo-check). It does not authorize a new ads budget, paid lists, outbound sending, or an agency-like fulfillment team.

## Buyer qualification before channel selection

The early customer is a **software business founder** with direct or delegated authority over a source-controlled acquisition website, a credible problem attracting appropriate visitors, and enough value at stake to make a repeated workflow worth purchasing. A named company profile or a count of site visitors is not evidence of purchase intent by itself.

| Buyer stage | Evidence available | First urgent problem to test | Disqualifying or delayed cases |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product not yet receiving search traffic | Public product/site, source code, small or no Search Console sample | Important pages are discoverable and technically sound; first commercially relevant page plan | Wants traffic guarantees or fabricated search trends |
| Some relevant search impressions / inbound traffic | Query/page trends, high-intent URLs, founder-known customers | Choose and ship a valuable existing-page improvement with much less founder effort | Cannot approve or merge changes; cannot connect sufficient evidence |
| Software with meaningful organic contribution | Valuable traffic and signup/trial definition, higher cost of technical errors | Reliable recurring discovery, prioritization, supported changes and conversion integrity | Expects every channel and arbitrary code writes immediately |
| Growing founder-led software company | Multiple pages/products, supported destinations, real acquisition workload and budget | Recurring acquisition program with review cadence, verified execution and credible business observation | Needs an entirely custom staffed agency or generalized CRM replacement |

**The expansion customer is the original founder's growing company**, but the plan must also test whether larger customers with the same job can be acquired directly. Avoid assuming a pre-traffic founder will automatically mature into a high-ACV account or that a broad enterprise procurement motion is appropriate for the first product.

### Qualifying questions for outreach or onboarding

What is the last consequential acquisition decision the founder made? Which page or search query was involved? What tool, developer or consultant did they use? What did it cost in founder attention? Which source and publishing permissions are available? What is the meaningful qualified behavior: an activated trial, a demo with the right buyer, or paid signup? Will they pay for recurring, reviewed completion? Do they want Grow, Protect, or neither?

Use actual examples and authorized artifacts, not hypothetical “would you use an AI growth agent?” interest.

## One measurable funnel

Keep **marketing funnel**, **product activation**, **paid conversion**, **delivery and outcome** separate:

```text
qualified founder encounters Relay
    → arrives with a clear acquisition job
    → connects / authorizes an actual website and relevant source
    → gets one source-backed decision worth considering
    → approves or declines a supported improvement
    → observes verified production change (when eligible)
    → accepts a bounded paid Grow or Protect program
    → returns for another useful weekly review
    → renews or expands because more of their acquisition work is finished
```

An early pre-traffic founder can activate through a **credible read-only diagnosis** or justified no-action recommendation even when no GitHub edit is safe. Mark this as *research/diagnostic activation*, **not** as verified acquisition execution. Only a fresh independently verified live postcondition completes the supported website-change job. A paid program may retain with reliable review and protection coverage even when no edit is justified.

Never conflate:
- An upstream OpenSEO research-credit purchase with a paid Grow or Protect subscription.
- An API request, report, scheduled job, website visit, or generated draft with founder review and accepted useful work.
- Merged code with observed customer deployment.
- A successful content change with incremental acquired customers.
- An authorized user account with an independently paying customer.

## Channels to test

### 1. Developer-led product entry

The existing [Relay SEO Check GitHub Action](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay-seo-check) is a plausible low-friction discovery channel because it runs in the repository workflow that developers already trust. Do not infer referral traffic or conversion merely from installations.

Test one contextual link from an actual supported check outcome to an explanation of how Relay can investigate a finding and prepare a source-mapped, approved PR. Respect GitHub permissions, project privacy, and the difference between a free CI check and a subscription. Measure the channel from qualified developers arriving, through authorized connection and first useful action, to payment and repeat use.

Avoid forcing a broad OAuth install or payment before someone can understand what the tool does. Reuse an already authorized repo and explain additional scope before requesting it.

### 2. Founder-led technical demonstrations

Publish substantive demonstrations of a real acquisition job: original search or audit evidence; why the founder chose this page; the precise proposed diff; the PR and independent production verification; the time and review effort; and what later measurements do or do not establish.

Use **Playbook-owned properties for engineering demonstrations**, clearly identified as internal. External customer case studies require written approval, permission to publish source/website evidence, and defensible numbers. Never turn a delayed search result into a guaranteed customer acquisition claim.

This channel tests whether precise, inspectable execution earns enough trust to obtain the next customer, not whether posting content produces large impression counts. Shots can produce supporting visuals and publishing assets, while Relay remains responsible for accurately describing its own workflow.

### 3. Focused founder discovery and sales

Recruit a narrow sample of independent founders who operate GitHub-managed websites and can describe a recent actual acquisition decision. Compare the current substitute directly: Search Console and a coding assistant, an SEO contractor, incumbent research software, an agency, or doing nothing.

Run a paid, capped design-partner offer with disclosed sources, reviewer authority, supported edit classes and budget. Track time-to-first-value and the reason a founder does not give source permission. Avoid high-volume unsolicited outreach as the default distribution motion.

The aim is to learn which segment values the service *before* attempting to automate prospecting, rather than letting the existence of Verify or Warmbly decide the sales channel.

### 4. Partnerships, only after one repeatable direct motion

Developer infrastructure, design/website consultancies, technical founder communities and specialized fractional growth practitioners might help Relay reach businesses with relevant sites. Partners may instead create high-touch support and reduce margin. Test partner-attributed activation, payer identity, account ownership, servicing costs and retention separately from direct customers.

Don't turn agency portfolios or generic SMB marketing into the default segment merely because a partner introduces them.

## Commercial metric definitions

All metrics must be computed on identified, scoped accounts and a stated observation period. A user or installation is not a customer. Keep founder/research/Grow/Protect/other program revenue separate.

| Measure | Definition | Do not count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Qualified account** | A founder-led software product with a supported acquisition job and identifiable decision owner | Anonymous visitors, generic SEO curiosity or unattributed bot installations |
| **First useful decision** | The authorized user reviews source-backed evidence, accepts a next action, or explicitly approves a justified no-action result | An automatically generated score or report without review |
| **Verified first change** | Founder-approved supported edit independently checked at the intended production destination | Draft, open PR, merge, preview or founder-only unverified attestation |
| **Paid acquisition program** | A real customer paid for the defined Grow/Protect program with accepted scope and unit | Free account, research credits or uncollected pricing commitment |
| **Repeat usage** | A second eligible evidence-backed decision cycle involving an authorized person | Another background cron tick or repeated same suggestion |
| **Paid retention** | Paid accounts/ARR remaining in an eligible cohort at renewal | Active product logins of unpaid users |
| **Expansion** | Actual increased contracted recurring revenue for the same account | Usage overage, speculative intended upgrade or an unrelated new customer |
| **CAC** | Attributable acquisition marketing/sales spend plus acquisition-specific staff/assistance cost ÷ newly acquired paying customers in a defined cohort | Unaccounted founder hours or “free” engineering demos |
| **Gross profit per account** | Collected recurring revenue less attributable product delivery costs: research/LLM provider, rendering, verification/hosting, and routine support labor | Marketing CAC (tracked separately), fictional traffic lift |
| **CAC payback** | CAC ÷ the account's monthly gross-profit contribution, with consistent billing cadence | Revenue-only denominator or negative/unknown costs treated as zero |

Break down channel and cohort economics: how many qualified founders entered the funnel; how many consented to the needed evidence and GitHub scope; how many got a useful decision; how many got a live verification; how many paid; how many returned; and how many renewed/expanded. Include absolute counts and dollars when cohorts are small.

**Illustrative arithmetic, not evidence:** If acquiring a paying customer consumes $600 of real attributable effort and the customer produces $240/month in gross-profit contribution, simple payback is 2.5 months. If hidden onboarding/support labor makes real monthly contribution only $60, it becomes 10 months. Both cases have the same nominal subscription. This is why labor must be visible.

## An evidence-gated early distribution program

The windows below start when an explicit test begins. They are not promised calendar dates, growth targets already achieved, or reasons to spend before the first workflow is proven.

| Decision stage | Work | Proof to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customer and message** | Interview a focused sample of founder-led software buyers, observe their actual substitutes and test a specific paid promise | Buyer/trigger, stated price, accepted or refused permissions, credible example and reasons for no purchase |
| **One working entry channel** | Test technical discovery via a developer utility or accurate founder demonstration | Source-tagged qualified arrival → authorized first useful decision; intervention and cost logged |
| **Paid acquisition** | Convert a bounded founder pilot and run an M13-backed use case | Payment versus promise, scope, first value and verified technical completion (if supported), cost to acquire and deliver |
| **Repeat / renewal** | Observe several actual review cycles, good no-action outcomes and paid account renewal | Second-use and retention by cohort, actual staff/provider cost, customer feedback and cancellations |
| **Expansion and repeatability** | Test whether a revenue-generating founder-led business pays for additional useful coverage | Observed upsell/NRR, higher-capacity workload, risk/permission model, measured costs and self-serve-versus-assisted differences |

**Promotion gate:** Do not increase spending on an acquisition channel unless the current evidence shows at least one qualified paid conversion path, measured fully loaded cost and signs of repeated customer value. A small sample can justify another experiment, not a reliable CAC or $100M revenue forecast.

**Failure conditions:** weak lead quality; too much unauthorized access; founders not returning after one edit; SEO evidence too sparse; low willingness to pay; churn around slow measurement windows; paid provider or manual support costs that erase contribution; or expansion work that duplicates a specialized Playbook product. Stop, narrow or reprice; don't respond by producing more generic content.

## Distribution and Playbook product boundaries

- **Relay** produces evidence-backed website acquisition recommendations, source-controlled execution and destination/outcome records. It also owns sales of its own recurring acquisition program.
- **Shots** produces product visuals and release communications where requested, but a Shorts/social post is not automatically proof of Relay acquisition.
- **Verify/Warmbly** can later provide consented outbound work if a distinct paid demand and safe handoff justify it. There is no implicit access to their mailboxes, profiles, suppression lists or sending credentials.
- **Playbook Operator** may someday coordinate across apps. It does not replace explicit account identity, consent, budget or user approval between them.

The M18 cross-product handoff is **not a prerequisite** for proving Relay distribution. Internal cross-promotion from Playbook products is a channel experiment, not evidence of product-market fit.
