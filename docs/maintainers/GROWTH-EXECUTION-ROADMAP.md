# Relay growth execution roadmap — founder-focused direction

**Strategy authority:** [vision.md](../../vision.md), updated November 7, 2026.  
**Audience:** independent founders of software businesses with source-controlled acquisition websites.  
**Execution evidence:** [existing RFC inventory and IRFC traceability](RFC-MILESTONE-TRACEABILITY.md).  
**Status:** product re-prioritization and company-scale expansion thesis, not proof that planned features, paid retention or acquisition lift exist.

## Decision

Relay's first outcome is **an evidence-backed organic-acquisition opportunity that an independent founder approves, ships through their own website, verifies live, and subsequently observes**. Optimize for completing that loop, not for owning release operations, managing every product fact, duplicating Shots, or adding more SEO dashboards.

Release regression protection still matters. The existing Protect engineering slices remain valid and can be completed when they support acquisition reliability or a separately validated Protect offer. They are **not** a universal prerequisite for an already supported SEO opportunity → GitHub change → measurement workflow.

Do not abandon the inherited OpenSEO services, change approvals, site audits, GSC/GA4 integrations, DataForSEO budgets, experiment history or dual-database safety model. Do not recreate finished historical RFC work to satisfy new milestone labels.

## The bigger ambition and why the roadmap changes

Relay is aiming for a recurring customer-acquisition execution business that could eventually support $100M+ ARR. The existing SEO research and safe GitHub publishing work is an initial wedge, not the complete product. The company must win four larger jobs: **ongoing commercially relevant work, repeat weekly usage, pricing that expands as a founder-led software business grows, and reliable distinctions between shipped code and acquired customers**.

The founder remains the initial customer. The scale test is whether the same founder-led company keeps paying for a more consequential recurring acquisition program as its website, funnels, products, users and purchase budget grow. A low-priced keyword tool, unlimited human SEO consultancy or omnichannel marketing suite is not the plan.

A **paid acquisition program** has an authorized site/product, intended buyer, qualified event definition, source coverage, approved actions, recurring review cadence, spend ceiling, destination checks and service owner. Its weekly review can end in a justified no-action decision. A cron tick and another generated article are not proof of useful recurring work.

The new [acquisition execution company-scale plan](ACQUISITION-EXECUTION-SCALE-PLAN.md) specifies the program contract, proposed subscription tiers, evidence clocks, commercial gates and scale economics.

### What actually has to be demonstrated

- **M13:** a specific opportunity reaches production safely and is technically verified. This is an engineering proof point, not proof of increased signups.
- **M7 and M17:** founders authorize the job, pay a disclosed price and return or renew; larger accounts expand only when they see additional useful work and cost-effective service.
- **M15:** multiple weekly acquisition review and completion cycles are genuinely useful without a quota of artificial edits.
- **M16:** a founder can define qualified trials/customers, trace permitted sources and tell what shipped versus what users did, with uncertainty and attribution assumptions intact.
- **M18:** only after recurring paid demand, one additional channel can contribute under an approved handoff and verifiable receipt.

**Financial arithmetic is not market evidence:** $100M ARR could mean approximately 10,000 accounts averaging $833 per month or 5,000 accounts averaging $1,667. Do not plan hiring, infrastructure, or marketing spend based on that arithmetic without demonstrated willingness to pay and sustainable acquisition/retention.

## A practical first growth execution slice (M13)

### Founder setup

- Establish product URL, target customer, high-value pages and one conversion event, using the existing project/settings structure.
- Connect GitHub and GSC where authorized; optional GA4 and DataForSEO research are bounded and disclosed.
- If Search Console has no usable history, report the limitation and offer a small site-audit/public-demand path, **not** an invented traffic forecast.

### Select work

- Start with a clearly relevant existing-page opportunity: weak title/snippet, missing important answer, underperforming query, confirmed indexability problem, or a supported source-backed copy issue.
- Show the baseline, evidence date, confidence, why it matters for the founder's audience, cost to investigate, expected technical postcondition and risk.
- Reuse opportunity detectors and existing review. Do not require all Protect monitoring snapshots or cross-system release facts before an ordinary SEO proposal.

### Execute and verify

- Require supported Next.js/static/known-source adapter and exact source mapping.
- Show the immutable diff and separate approval to open PR. Customer retains merge/deploy authority.
- Pin exact revision, observe deployment where reliable, independently inspect the live destination and record blocked/inconclusive states.
- Open an existing Search Console experiment or equivalent observation only with sufficient data and the established 28-day baseline, 7-day exclusion and 28-day observation window where applicable. Keep search-indexing lag and causal uncertainty visible.

### Commercial acceptance

- Demonstrate the flow on a Playbook site first for engineering confidence; external paid independent-founder evidence is still required.
- Track founder setup time, provider calls and costs, time to reviewed proposal, time to independently verified production state, false proposals and actual renewal behavior.
- **The initial promise is reliable completion of approved website work, not guaranteed growth in clicks or revenue.**

Tracking issues: [#265 onboarding and first recommendation](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/265), [#266 first verified growth change](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/266), [#267 paid pilot comparison](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/267).

## M13 delivery specification: one trustworthy founder-growth result

The work below is the smallest integrated job worth putting in front of an independent founder. It reuses capabilities in [RFC-0002](rfcs/0002-opportunity-detectors.md), [RFC-0003](rfcs/0003-opportunity-queue.md), [RFC-0004](rfcs/0004-ai-seo-engineer.md), [RFC-0005](rfcs/0005-github-execution.md), [RFC-0006](rfcs/0006-outcome-measurement.md), and existing GitHub proposal/experiments docs. It is **not** authorization to create new providers, a generic growth agent or an alternate persistence model.

### Vertical slices and required proof

| Slice | Reuse first | Change only when needed | Acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **E0 — Founder intent and authorization** | Existing projects, source permissions, GSC connection, setup UX | Capture intended buyer, high-value URLs and one selected conversion definition without forcing all integrations | Authorized site/source; revoked/missing consent is clearly blocked; new site can proceed to limited read-only audit |
| **E1 — Credible opportunity** | DataForSEO, opportunity detection, Search Console, site audits, review queue | Explain page/query fit, evidence window, freshness, cost and technical actionability; handle no-data and no-action outcomes | Founder's product problem and a specific source-backed recommendation, not fabricated volume/ROI |
| **E2 — Supported exact proposal** | Publishing file locator, narrow metadata/copy/Markdown diff, current-source checks and approval | Show explicit postcondition and make unsupported targets recoverable via ticket | Exact repo/path/base revision/source fingerprint; stale/dynamic/missing claims are refused |
| **E3 — Founder-owned execution** | GitHub PR creation, CI checks, execution leases/idempotence | Improve progression UI and duplicate/stale recovery without bypassing the owner | One approved PR and correct state through merge; no automatic deployment |
| **E4 — Live destination proof** | Existing deployment reads and experiment sources, site snapshots and SEO rule checks | Add the *specific* independently observed destination check that is missing for the supported v1 edit | Timestamped live URL/version/postcondition; a merged PR or passing preview cannot count as success |
| **E5 — Honest follow-up and memory** | Experiments, Search Console/GA4 windows, This Week, activity and spend history | Join the approved change, live verification and later observations into the same founder-visible work item | Correct 28/7/28-day eligibility and insufficient-data behavior; manual steps clearly labelled until automated |

Implement E0–E5 as a single customer-visible slice rather than building generic schemas for unrelated future products. Code can be staged in small PRs, but the release gate is a real authorized **end-to-end** flow.

### The data-availability decision tree

| Starting situation | Safe next action | Unsupported claim to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GitHub connection and GSC property both available, sufficient first-party query/page history | Select one high-intent opportunity from actual page/query performance | Estimated revenue or causal ranking improvement |
| Site accessible but GSC history sparse, zero or not connected | Run bounded public technical audit and optional paid external keyword investigation with cost confirmation | Pretending that external keyword volumes are the site's traffic or that query CTR exists |
| GSC connected but page identity/canonical differs | Explain property, URL and attribution mismatch; require review | Automatically writing to a guessed page |
| Source route dynamic or generated, or code-to-render mapping ambiguous | Show finding and safe ticket; request file/owner clarification | Arbitrary file editing or copying rendered text into source |
| GitHub PR merged but deployment cannot be tied to the changed page | Show merged / deployment unknown; offer authorized checks | Successful deployment or technical resolution |
| Live change verified, but measurement window still incomplete | Show technical completion and scheduled or explicit later observation | Claiming improved customer acquisition |
| Founder rejects or defers an edit | Preserve reason and cooldown, provide alternate next best action or no-action | Recommending the same rejected edit again immediately |

### Acceptance scenarios to run on a real test property

1. **Positive path:** connect an authorized GitHub-managed Next.js site and evidence source; select a real page-level opportunity; prepare an exact safe edit; approve the PR; allow customer CI/merge/deploy; independently fetch the intended production URL and confirm the original technical postcondition. Link evidence, timestamps, revisions and reviewer/owner.
2. **No history:** connect a new but authorized site with no usable Search Console query data. Show source freshness and a useful bounded audit or explicit no-action result; do not synthesize traffic or false click trends.
3. **Source refusal:** choose an unsupported computed/dynamic file or stale base SHA. No write occurs; the founder sees why, which file/rule needs review and how to create a ticket.
4. **Delivery uncertainty:** simulate merge before deployment, delayed production caching, and an independent fix by the founder. Only the correct final live page can earn technical verification and Relay must not claim credit for a separate fix.
5. **Measurement uncertainty:** use a changed page without enough baseline data, incomplete post-indexing windows or simultaneous other changes. Preserve an inconclusive, observational result and the next eligible check.
6. **Security and cost:** revoke a source connection, attempt cross-project access, exhaust a spend cap and retry an interrupted approved PR write. Rejections must be correct, auditable, limited and idempotent.

For every scenario, record which statements were proven on a real site, which used fixtures, which provider calls were paid and what manual operator work remained. No production-seeded harmful regression is required to exercise the Growth v1 opportunity case.

### Explicitly blocked scope for E0–E5

No large Product Reality Engine schema, multi-platform content scheduling, agent-sent campaigns, global competitor crawling, bulk page generation, direct CRM mutations, multi-tenant repository scans without consent, automatic PR merge or hosted execution of customer-supplied code. Add an adapter only when the founder's particular website makes an approved safe outcome impossible without it, and keep its security and cost review separate.

## Commercial experiment design

M7 is not a decorative sales milestone. Founder revenue is the constraint on whether M10 or M14 deserves significant engineering investment.

**Grow proposal:** a fixed-scope, repeatable website acquisition review and a capped number of supported reviewed site changes. A pricing test must account for provider calls, personal review, tech-support time and slow measurement windows; do not advertise unlimited SEO work.

**Protect proposal:** explicit selected pages, check frequency, coverage health, incident confirmation and human-approved technical repair. This may be valuable to an established site that can lose substantial acquisition traffic, but its buyer may differ from a pre-traffic founder. Do not count the two offers as the same demand signal.

Compare both offers using reconstructed *actual* customer workflows, specific account permissions, observed trial execution and actual paid outcomes. Ask whether the founder would rather pay Relay, keep the existing research/coding setup, or pay a consultant. Record the competitor/substitute they actually use, not a hypothetical category.

### Decision record for each pilot

Each pilot should answer: the founder's problem and current baseline; a responsible buyer; the supported scope; initial setup and approval friction; one delivered job or an honest blocked/no-action result; actual external provider spend and human hours; later observation completeness; whether payment and renewal occurred; and whether the customer requested another comparable job without prompting.

The stop/go test should be capable of saying **no**. If a founder will not grant narrow source access, opportunities aren't useful, the service needs extensive manual rewriting, or support/verification costs erase margin, reduce scope or change the wedge before adding connectors. Playbook's own website success cannot replace independent paying-customer evidence.

## The second loop: distributing Relay itself

M13 proves an acquisition website change can be completed safely for a founder. M15/M16 test recurring work and real qualified demand. Those engineering outcomes still leave a company-level question: **can Relay repeatedly acquire suitable paying founders at acceptable cost?**

Under [M17](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/milestone/17), [issue #280](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/280) and the [Founder Acquisition & Distribution plan](FOUNDER-ACQUISITION-DISTRIBUTION.md), test the public GitHub SEO Check and founder-led technical demonstrations as **hypotheses**, not established acquisition channels. Follow qualified arrivals to authorized product activation, useful supported decisions, verified work when eligible, actual paid acquisition programs, second-cycle use, renewal and account expansion. A GitHub install and a $10 research subscriber do not count as a paid Grow or Protect customer.

CAC must include attributable acquisition staff/founder hours as well as out-of-pocket spending; payback uses gross-profit contribution after ordinary provider and support costs. Report self-serve and assisted conversions separately. The early product cannot claim sustainable software economics if every approved site change requires manual Playbook intervention.

**Promotion rule:** expand acquisition spending or distribution partnerships only after a source-backed paid cohort shows useful repeat work, measured full costs and a plausible payback period. If founder acquisition is harder or more expensive than the product can support, reduce support obligations or narrow the customer rather than promising a larger platform.

## Transition from the first site change to a recurring acquisition program

The roadmap must not end when a website change is live. After M13, the product should know **what the founder is trying to acquire, which important work was considered, what reached the site, and what next week's data allows us to conclude**.

### M15: weekly decision and repeatable execution

Use the current This Week, opportunity, approval, run history and experiment components. Add an agreed acquisition goal, source freshness, current stage, review owner and why work was selected or deferred. Preserve every explicitly rejected idea and cooldown. The first small repeatable playbooks should use already supported Next.js metadata/copy changes and existing protection diagnoses rather than a generic autonomous writer.

**Proof:** at least two different useful acquisition review cycles on a real authorized site; four weekly windows exercised for scheduling/refusals, with manual vs synthetic time distinguished. A no-action week should be legitimate when source coverage is healthy. Track founder review minutes, accepted actions, provider spend and repeat visits.

### M16: outcome accounting before bigger promises

A founder should identify qualified signup/trial/demo/activation goals and, where consented, paid conversion or pipeline source. Reuse GSC, GA4 and existing read-only attribution rather than replicating analytics. The record should distinguish `verified technical completion`, `observed exposure`, `qualified behavior` and `observed economics`.

**Proof:** one source-checked acquisition funnel with complete definitions, data-age coverage and honest missingness. A merged PR that never deployed is not a completed action; a completed action is not proof of incremental sales; first-touch billing labels are not causal attribution.

### M17: contracts worth renewing and expanding

Compare concrete paid offers rather than debating hypothetical tiers. The proposal ranges are founder initial **$199–$499/month**, recurring growth **$750–$2,500/month**, and a scaled founder-led program **$3,000–$8,000+** only where the business stakes and delivered service justify it. These are experiments, not active plans or forecast ARPA.

**Proof:** payment, renewal and actual tier expansion recorded with program scope and provider/compute/human support cost. Segment research-credit users, Grow customers, Protect customers and any assisted work. When evidence is weak, narrow or pause the higher tier rather than selling undeliverable commitments.

### M18: expand responsibility only through source-backed handoffs

The next channel is chosen by a real paying buyer's recurring problem, not a desire for 78 connectors. Website discovery, selected AI visibility and conversion checks already give enough surface to test M13/M15/M16. If distribution, partner outreach, or targeted outbound deserves an integration, the system that already owns creative or sending retains authority.

**Proof:** named customer goal, permission matrix, bounded versioned payload, idempotent request, verified destination state and observably relevant acquisition metric. Do not ship paid ad buying, blanket outbound or raw CRM ingestion as a speculative connector release.

### Decision dependencies

**M13 + paid M7 discovery** start now. M15 builds recurring review/work after M13 is viable. Minimum M16 event definitions can proceed alongside it when an authorized customer provides actual data. M17 pricing research begins alongside pilots and formalizes higher-value packages only after a repeat-use signal. M18 requires the repeat paid program, defensible outcomes and buyer consent. General Product Reality Engine and Relay-owned Launch stay off this critical path.

## Portfolio boundary and future integrations

Relay may read an approved product description or documentation source to avoid inventing features on an SEO page. That does not make it the owner of product launch work. The first cross-product handoff, if useful, should be a small approved request or link to **Shots** for screenshots, launch announcements and social-ready assets. Verify/Warmbly owns contact quality and outbound. A future Playbook Operator may coordinate their tasks; do not build a competing general agent supervisor in Relay.
