## Why marketing has its own milestone

M13 proves that Relay can complete one supported website-acquisition change for a founder. M15/M16 address recurring work and observed acquisition value. M17 evaluates company pricing, paid cohorts, CAC and retention. **M19 is the marketing execution program that brings the right founders into that product experience.** It should be a separate owner and queue so that publishing activity is never mistaken for product delivery or commercial proof.

The first job is to help one independently operated software company understand what Relay actually does and take a credible first step: discover a relevant acquisition problem, review a supported site improvement, and see how the founder retains control of the source change. We should market the mechanism and limitations faithfully rather than claim autonomous SEO, guaranteed rankings, or a proven customer-growth effect.

Do not assume a paid advertising budget. Initially prioritize the existing developer utility, founder-led demonstrations, useful technical material, and focused community participation. All campaigns must have an identifiable founder audience and a specific next action.

## Positioning and message architecture

**Category:** founder-focused website acquisition execution, beginning with organic search and evidence-backed, source-controlled changes.

**Primary audience:** an independent software founder who owns or can approve a GitHub-managed acquisition site, needs qualified inbound demand, and lacks capacity to continuously research, implement, ship and measure SEO improvements.

**Secondary audience (test, not default):** founder-led SaaS with existing organic traffic where frequent deployments make broken acquisition pages expensive. Protect is a distinct hypothesis and should not be bundled into an imaginary product already for sale.

**Main message to test:** "Find the next worthwhile website improvement. Review the exact change. Ship it through your repository. See what actually went live."

**Proof:** connect actual dated page/query evidence or supported website check to a narrow proposed GitHub diff; show approval, customer-controlled merge/deploy and independent technical verification. Search/AI visibility, qualified signups and revenue are subsequent observations, with appropriate limits.

**Don't claim:** guaranteed rankings, customer acquisition, traffic lift, autonomous deployment, arbitrary CMS/framework support, fully automated GSC experiments, customer logos/testimonials without authorization, or general Sync/Launch features from retained historical RFCs.

| Audience situation | Message to test | Evidence a founder needs | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Newly launched, little search history | "Know whether important pages can be discovered and what to investigate next" | Honest bounded technical diagnostics and source/date of research, no fabricated clicks | Explore existing safe check or founder review |
| Early organic search traffic | "Stop turning search recommendations into an engineering backlog" | Query/page observation, supported exact diff, approval and production check | Review one qualified acquisition opportunity |
| Material traffic / frequent releases | "Protect important acquisition pages without surrendering source control" | Declared page/cadence, supported regression evidence and explicit repair authority | Discuss bounded Protect pilot |
| Revenue-generating software founder | "Make website acquisition a recurring job you can review, authorize and measure" | Repeat verified work and qualified event coverage; not invented attribution | Discuss a scoped paid acquisition program |

## The first bounded channel experiments

### Experiment A — Developer utility to first useful Relay action

Audit the real SEO Check action, its README, public checks and any current site links. Do not assume a Marketplace listing or referral flow exists. Produce one accurate example that begins with a meaningful site/PR finding and shows how the founder would investigate and approve a supported correction in Relay. If the product cannot complete the path yet, describe it as a planned capability and route to a narrowly scoped design-partner conversation instead of falsely claiming automation.

**Observe:** qualified action visitors → Relay marketing landing visits → explicit sign-in/authorized connection → reviewed relevant recommendation → paid pilot where offered. Confirm data provenance; an action installation or GitHub star is not payment.

### Experiment B — Proof-led technical founder content

Choose one permitted real example from a Playbook website or consenting customer. Record the observed problem, source and query/date, proposed and approved diff, PR result, production observation and later measurement eligibility. Build one detailed, useful post or recorded walkthrough. Repurpose it into short accurate founder-facing explanations through existing owned channels (X/LinkedIn and suitable community participation); use Shots for approved creative production if it helps. Do not create a fictional protagonist, testimonial or positive result.

**Observe:** relevant source-tagged visits, founder replies, authorized first-use decisions and meaningful conversations. Likes and impressions are secondary.

### Experiment C — Focused founder discovery

Identify actual software founders who have discussed a relevant website/search acquisition problem or are personally reachable through existing relationships. Ask about the last completed website improvement, time/effort, approval limitations and what they currently pay for. Offer a bounded, honestly described design-partner workflow with a specific owner and approved scope. Respect community rules and outreach consent; do not automate cold messages by default.

**Observe:** qualified conversations, source access decisions, actual paid offer acceptance, second-cycle interest and reasons the founder's current workflow is good enough.

## Minimal weekly operating cadence

This is a **capacity guideline**, not a mandatory content quota. One person must be able to operate it without a new marketing department.

- **Collect evidence:** review a product change, real site finding, founder objection or completed supported demo. No evidence means no fabricated story.
- **Choose one claim:** who is the reader, what exactly is verified, which founder problem matters and what we want them to do next.
- **Produce and publish:** start with one substantive technical walkthrough or useful explanation; adapt only when the source material supports alternate formats. Use existing Shots/schedulers and approved accounts.
- **Have a real conversation:** respond to useful founder questions, verify actual purchase/substitute objections and avoid mass unrequested outreach.
- **Review the funnel:** log source, qualified founder arrival, first useful action, pilot offer, payment if any, acquisition-specific staff time, what failed and which experiment to stop or repeat.

Avoid scheduling an arbitrary number of posts merely to show activity. If one source-backed demonstration creates five genuinely useful formats, publish them. If it doesn't, don't force five posts.

## Marketing measurement versus company economics

The marketing scorecard should preserve **actual count, date, cohort, attribution quality and cost**:

| Stage | Marketing measurement | Reporting rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach | Qualified founder-facing views or visits, by source/asset | Bots, unrelated readers, unknown source separately labelled |
| Interest | CTA actions, relevant conversations and explicit permission questions | An email open or content like is not a lead |
| Activation | Authorized site/project plus one genuinely reviewed, useful Relay decision | Demo content or merely created account does not qualify |
| Execution | Founder-approved source change independently verified live where eligible | PR created/merged is not enough |
| Purchase | Actual paid Grow or Protect acquisition program | Research credits/free plans excluded |
| Retention | Second meaningful review cycle and actual paid renewal | No artificial recommendations in a quiet week |
| Cost | Content time, outreach/sales time and direct expenses | Founder hours are not zero-cost |

M19 owns marketing campaigns and the conversion funnel through paid-program handoff. **M17 owns price, recurring revenue, gross/retained ARR, CAC/payback, account expansion and contribution margin** using the same sourced cohort records, including marketing acquisition time. Do not create a second analytics database or redefine payment.

## Exit criteria and explicit failure

M19's initial campaign experiment is complete when:
1. The first buyer/message is clear and published truthfully or documented as blocked by product readiness.
2. At least one identifiable distribution path has been operated and reviewed with qualified founder evidence, not just content volume.
3. Attribution from that source to a genuine first-use action is documented to the extent permitted, with refusals/missing data explicit.
4. A paid founder pilot was offered on truthful terms where appropriate; payment and nonpayment are reported honestly, not required to manufacture a win.
5. The actual acquisition-specific cost and founder time are recorded; a decision states which channel to repeat, revise or stop.

**Marketing does not need invented positive results to close an experiment.** If no paying founders convert, report it and change the offer, channel or targeting before spending more. If M13 cannot reliably perform the promised website change, narrow the headline and sell an assisted pilot honestly rather than claiming a finished autonomous growth product.

The next investment after the first experiment is decided jointly with M13 technical evidence and M17 buyer economics. The milestone is a work-and-learning boundary, not a substitute for customer demand.
