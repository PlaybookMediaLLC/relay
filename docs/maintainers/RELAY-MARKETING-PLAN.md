## Why marketing has its own milestone

M13 proves that Relay can complete one supported website-acquisition change for a founder. M15/M16 address recurring work and observed acquisition value. M17 evaluates company pricing, paid cohorts, CAC and retention. **M19 is the marketing execution program that brings the right founders into that product experience.** It should be a separate owner and queue so that publishing activity is never mistaken for product delivery or commercial proof.

The first job is to help one independently operated software company understand what Relay actually does and take a credible first step: discover a relevant acquisition problem, review a supported site improvement, and see how the founder retains control of the source change. We should market the mechanism and limitations faithfully rather than claim autonomous SEO, guaranteed rankings, or a proven customer-growth effect.

Do not assume a paid advertising budget. Initially prioritize the existing developer utility, founder-led demonstrations, useful technical material, and focused community participation. All campaigns must have an identifiable founder audience and a specific next action.

## Positioning and message architecture

**Category:** founder-focused website acquisition execution, beginning with organic search and evidence-backed, source-controlled changes.

**Primary audience:** an independent software founder who owns or can approve a GitHub-managed acquisition site, needs qualified inbound demand, and lacks capacity to continuously research, implement, ship and measure SEO improvements.

**Secondary audience (test, not default):** founder-led SaaS with existing organic traffic where frequent deployments make broken acquisition pages expensive. Protect is a distinct hypothesis and should not be bundled into an imaginary product already for sale.

**Main message to test:** "Find the next worthwhile website improvement. Review the exact change. Ship it through your repository. See what actually went live."

**Proof:** connect actual dated page/query evidence or supported website check to a narrow proposed GitHub diff; show approval, customer-controlled merge/deploy and independent technical verification. Search/AI visibility, qualified signups and revenue are subsequent observations, with appropriate limits.

**Don't claim:** guaranteed rankings, customer acquisition, traffic lift, autonomous deployment, arbitrary CMS/framework support, fully automated GSC experiments, customer logos/testimonials without authorization, or general Sync/Launch features from retained historical RFCs.

| Audience situation | Message to test | Evidence a founder needs | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Newly launched, little search history | "Know whether important pages can be discovered and what to investigate next" | Honest bounded technical diagnostics and source/date of research, no fabricated clicks | Explore existing safe check or founder review |
| Early organic search traffic | "Stop turning search recommendations into an engineering backlog" | Query/page observation, supported exact diff, approval and production check | Review one qualified acquisition opportunity |
| Material traffic / frequent releases | "Protect important acquisition pages without surrendering source control" | Declared page/cadence, supported regression evidence and explicit repair authority | Discuss bounded Protect pilot |
| Revenue-generating software founder | "Make website acquisition a recurring job you can review, authorize and measure" | Repeat verified work and qualified event coverage; not invented attribution | Discuss a scoped paid acquisition program |

## Social distribution: four platforms, four different jobs

This plan adds **[X #289](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/289), [Instagram #290](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/290), [YouTube #291](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/291), and [TikTok #292](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/292)** to M19. They are marketing experiments for **Relay itself**, not newly implemented publishing features inside Relay.

The business goal remains to reach founders of software products who can complete the first Relay growth workflow and eventually pay for a repeatable acquisition program. Four channels should **not** produce four disconnected editorial calendars. One verified product case or founder question provides a common source; each platform gets an appropriate independent treatment and its own measured audience.

### Role and initial format by platform

| Channel | Main job | Format to test | Audience action | Best learning signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **X** | Meet technical founders, clarify an opinion and earn conversations | Plainspoken short posts, specific code/source screenshots, one deeper thread when useful, substantive replies | Discuss an actual acquisition implementation problem, view a real technical example, ask about a bounded trial | Relevant builder replies, qualified conversations and attributable site actions |
| **YouTube** | Build evergreen trust by showing a technical job end-to-end | Full screen-led walkthrough and self-contained Shorts | Watch the implementation and review a truthful current Relay demo/pilot path | Retention on technical steps, qualified comments, relevant visits and first useful action |
| **Instagram** | Make the invisible technical work immediately understandable and show the founder behind it | Original Reels, visual diagnostic carousels and occasional Stories | Save/share a useful workflow, visit founder/product profile and follow a truthful CTA | Founder-relevant shares, saves, DMs and attributable activation |
| **TikTok** | Test which founder acquisition pains resonate beyond the developer audience | Original direct-to-camera problem diagnosis, short screen proof and responses to real questions | Recognize the problem, ask a relevant founder question and investigate Relay if qualified | Relevant comments/profile actions, useful conversations and attributable visits where possible |

**Keep the accounts and buyer separate:** the founder's personal voice can be the trusted narrator when the owner approves it, while the Relay brand is the product destination. This plan does not assume access to a named personal social account, a pre-existing product account, or a social API. Check actual account permissions, profile links, posting features and native insight availability before operating them.

### Content system: one evidence source, four native treatments

Each source item is a compact **story dossier**, not an auto-generated quota:
- **Source:** real search query/page evidence, public supported site check, approved internal Playbook example, founder objection, or authenticated customer source with explicit publishing permission.
- **Finding:** what was actually observed, when, through which provider, for which page/audience, and whether data was complete.
- **Decision:** why a founder should or should not act. Include the current substitute and the smallest supported action, not a broad AI promise.
- **Technical receipt:** exact supported proposed diff, approval state, PR revision, actual deployment and independently observed production postcondition, when available.
- **Outcome boundaries:** technical completion, later descriptive search/traffic changes and paid/qualified conversion observations must appear as distinct statements. Unknown and no-action outcomes are valid.
- **Publishing rights:** customer consent, allowed screenshots, private data redaction, music/media rights, AI/synthetic disclosure and approved CTA.
- **Channel record:** asset ID, source dossier ID, platform, owner/account, asset format, caption/title, posting timestamp, link/UTM where available, estimated editing time and observed funnel state.

An evidence dossier can become:
1. **YouTube:** the full walkthrough with actual source, decision, limitations, on-screen diff and live check.
2. **X:** one sharply reasoned observation and an optional source-linked thread for technical founders, with genuine follow-up replies.
3. **Instagram:** a visual Reel/carousel showing what was broken, the specific safe change, and what became verifiable.
4. **TikTok:** a separately edited natural-language explanation focused on the founder's decision, with quick code/site proof when helpful.

Adapt the pacing, introduction, subtitles and framing to the viewer; don't export a watermarked clip four times or repeat the same thread as a teleprompter script. Use Shots for approved capture/visual assembly and existing scheduling capabilities if configured, but never silently cross-post using unverified credentials or assume every platform supports identical API/media behavior.

### Shared editorial pillars

**Founder decision problems:** "Which Search Console query actually deserves an edit?"; "Why a page can show impressions without attracting the right buyer"; "When a new site has insufficient data." Make the business judgment understandable without pretending to know conversion lift.

**Source-controlled execution:** why a source-mapped metadata fix can be harder than generating new copy; how a GitHub proposal protects the founder; what happens when dynamic routes, stale source, claims or permissions make an edit unsafe.

**Production and measurement truth:** merged PR versus deployed page, verified metadata versus changed Google snippets, enough Search Console history versus inadequate data, attribution labels versus acquired customers. These are strong demonstrations even when the final answer is inconclusive.

**Public building and founder dialogue:** explain actual code decisions, experiments, rejected work, pricing objections and founder workflow research. Never claim a fictitious founder asked a question. Use a specific real question with permission, or clearly state that a scenario is illustrative.

**CTA ladder:** a viewer who is curious can watch a truthful walkthrough; a technical founder with a real problem can inspect the website or the developer utility; an eligible founder can request a bounded demo/pilot where offered. Don't force every post to sell. Confirm the specific destination actually exists and is live before promoting it.

## Platform execution plans

### X — trusted technical conversations, not a posting bot

**Customer problem to own:** a founder knows their website should perform better but cannot tell which SEO recommendation is worth spending engineering time on. Technical founders on X can judge an exact code example, challenge assumptions and identify whether the current tool chain suffices.

**Native content:** short claims supported by one real observation, a mini source diff or actual failure state; occasional deeper threads that explain why the change was selected and where it could fail; real public conversation replies that answer the specific question. The founder account can provide context; the brand account, if used, should not mimic its owner or reply mechanically.

**First four-week test:** roughly 4–5 useful original posts weekly; up to one substantive thread from a verified case; 5–10 *genuine* relevant conversations when possible. This is a working hypothesis, not an X ranking rule or a minimum quota. Record the cost of writing and replying. Run two message hypotheses: implementation bottleneck versus protecting a revenue-critical acquisition page.

**Good opening structure:** establish a real visible contradiction or decision, show the relevant mechanism, state what can actually be verified, then invite a useful technical question. Do not use shock hooks, generic founder slogans or unsupported ROI math.

**Distribution:** make the founder/product profile and pinned explainer truthful. Link to one relevant current demonstration or scoped pilot. When responding to someone else's post, advance the conversation before linking a product. No automated engagement scraping or mass DMs.

**Readout:** meaningful builder replies, qualified site owners identified, profile/landing actions by tagged source where available, trials that reach a real founder decision and paid interest; likes/reposts are supporting attention signals only.

**Owner issue:** [#289](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/289). Reference: [X's community-management guide](https://business.x.com/en/basics/community-management), not a promise of reach.

### Instagram — visual product comprehension and founder credibility

**Customer problem to own:** an abstract "AI SEO engineer" is difficult to trust. Show the small concrete thing it actually changed, the proof the founder can inspect and the person responsible for the claims.

**Native content:** original founder-on-camera Reels with screen captures; annotated source/website carousels; occasional behind-the-scenes Stories or product questions. Use vertical-safe framing, subtitles and readable code/text. Screenshots must not reveal customer personal data, auth tokens, private domains or analytics without permission.

**First four-week test:** 2–3 original Reels and one useful carousel per week, with Stories when they support a real update or question. Test a specific "SEO report → code change" visual versus a "code merged → was it live?" diagnostic. Avoid artificially chopping one example into a daily quota.

**Story approach:** one observable issue in the opening, one short explanatory turn, visual proof, and a clear distinction between the change Relay completed and later outcomes that remain unmeasured. Carousels can show the fuller sequence when a video would make the source unreadable.

**Distribution:** check actual bio/profile CTA functionality. Use profile links and Stories options where available and avoid assuming every account has the same features. Reply to relevant founder messages manually. The platform-native Insights/Best Practices dashboard should inform format choices when accessible.

**Readout:** reach to relevant/nonfollowing people, watch time, shares/saves, meaningful founder DMs, profile actions and attributable product activations where measurable. Viral general-interest reach is not qualified SaaS-founder acquisition.

**Owner issue:** [#290](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/290). Reference: [Instagram creator best practices](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/amp/).

### YouTube — evergreen search-intent education and technical verification

**Customer problem to own:** an independent software founder wants to see the complete, inspectable acquisition workflow before trusting a tool with GitHub access.

**Native content:** one substantial screen-led episode that answers a specific founder question, plus self-contained Shorts with useful independent lessons. Suggested repeatable series: *The page we decided to change*, *What the search report didn't tell us*, *How we checked the live result*, and *When the data isn't enough*. These are editorial frames, not claims of published shows.

**First six-week test:** roughly one 8–15-minute, evidence-backed walkthrough every other week and two Shorts per week when source material supports them. Do not publish an invented end-to-end demo if M13 is incomplete; label manual or staged steps and publish a truthful subsystem tutorial instead. YouTube explicitly says there is no mandatory minimum Shorts posting cadence.

**Long-form structure:** show the real problem and page first; reconstruct the evidence and founder judgment; inspect the exact file/diff; clarify approval and customer-owned deployment; verify what actually appeared on production; discuss delayed/uncertain acquisition observations and costs. Add chapters, accurate source references, usable captions, descriptions and links to the supported product or design partner path.

**Shorts:** solve one narrow question without requiring viewers to already know Relay. One source-code failure or measurement misconception can be enough. Use clear captioning and readable mobile proof rather than a vertical crop that makes the editor illegible.

**Readout:** retention and audience fit for long-form, Shorts choose-to-view and retention, relevant founder comments, source-tagged visits and first useful product actions. Compare formats to their own baselines; watch time without the intended founder audience does not justify continued heavy editing.

**Owner issue:** [#291](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/291). References: [YouTube Shorts discovery](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225) and [Shorts content analytics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217).

### TikTok — test problems that resonate outside the developer bubble

**Customer problem to own:** technical founders may recognize they are losing time on SEO execution without seeking a GitHub-native marketing tool. Make the decision problem understandable before talking about infrastructure.

**Native content:** original direct-to-camera founder observations; quick screenshots/site/code demonstrations; honest disagreement with simplistic acquisition advice; occasional replies to genuine product/founder questions. Spoken explanations should sound like one experienced founder explaining one real problem, not a promotional essay.

**First four-week test:** around three original vertical clips per week, testing (A) problem-first versus (B) concrete visible-result-first openings. Start with 20–60 seconds when appropriate, but use a longer explanation if one technical point requires it. No claim that any length/cadence is algorithmically required.

**Distribution:** descriptions should name the actual search/acquisition problem in plain language. Avoid irrelevant trending audio or licensed media without permission. Test real profile/site link options; if no attributable link exists, record a profile/brand search action as qualitative or unattributed rather than claiming TikTok converted it.

**Readout:** retention/completion, meaningful founder questions, profile actions, qualified visits where observed and first useful Relay decisions. Document when wide views attract the wrong audience and reduce or change output accordingly.

**Owner issue:** [#292](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/292). Reference: [TikTok small-business creative guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/tiktok-creative-strategy-small-business), which is inspiration, not evidence of organic reach or B2B conversion.

## Cadence, ownership, and experiment design

A **four-to-six-week pilot** should prioritize proving the workflow and the qualified audience. The schedule below is a starting capacity test for one founder, not a contractual production target:

| Source work | Initial hypothesis | Owner or tool |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One real product/source dossier | One per week when factual evidence exists; obtain permission and truth check | Founder; existing Relay/GitHub evidence |
| Full YouTube walkthrough | One every two weeks | Founder voice + ScreenStudio or other existing screen recorder; approved edit |
| X posts | Four or five substantive posts per week and relevant non-spam replies | Founder; approved current account |
| Instagram | Two or three Reels plus one carousel weekly when useful | Founder + Shots for approved visuals |
| TikTok | Three independently edited native vertical clips weekly | Founder; no recycled watermarks |
| Shorts | Two self-contained YouTube Shorts weekly from available source | Founder + existing editing capability |
| Review | One short weekly channel and conversion/effort review | M19 #286 and #287; M17 handles actual CAC/retention |

**Allocation rule:** start by recording one genuinely useful long explanation or strong screen demonstration. X can be written from its observations; Instagram turns the proof into a visual sequence; TikTok turns the decision into an original spoken explanation; YouTube hosts the detailed context. If creating native adaptations consumes more time than it returns, narrow the number of active channels. Posting the same clip everywhere without differentiation is not a strategy.

### Six-week decision calendar

| Week | Primary work | Exit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0 — setup** | Audit actual accounts/bios/link capabilities, create source-tagged CTA destinations, map current product claims and permissions, select buyer segment | One approved profile/CTA and real proof dossier; identify missing functionality |
| **1–2 — channel hypotheses** | Publish first X explanatory content, one YouTube technical proof, Instagram visual sequence and TikTok founder diagnosis from the same source; test two distinct pain points | Actual assets, time spent, first relevant founder conversations, source coverage |
| **3–4 — evidence-based adaptation** | Repeat the best qualified founder problem; change only one major variable per channel (opening, format or CTA) where feasible | Comparison within similar formats, founder relevance, attributable activation/pilot action, no invented successes |
| **5–6 — consolidate** | Publish the next complete YouTube demo if evidence supports it, revisit founder questions, review channel economics and product activation blockers | Explicit keep/narrow/stop decisions per channel with qualified leads, product usefulness and time cost |

Four weeks of output is not enough to declare a stable customer acquisition channel or long-term retention. It is enough to discover weak positioning, content production burdens, wrong-audience virality, broken links and whether a real founder asks to try Relay. Longer measurement and renewals remain M17 decisions.

### Cross-platform scorecard and decision rights

Record **platform, approved account, evidence dossier ID, campaign/asset, format, published URL, date, audience hypothesis, actual minutes, direct costs, main CTA, link attribution status, audience-fit signal, qualified founder conversation, first useful product decision, offered pilot and actual paid conversion when available**. Use existing owned analytics; no new attribution warehouse or invasive viewer tracking is authorized.

Differentiate *attention* (views, watch time, shares), *intent* (founder questions/profile or link actions), *activation* (authorized site and reviewed useful decision), *execution* (independently verified live change), and *purchase* (real Grow/Protect paid agreement). An X reply, Instagram share, YouTube subscriber and TikTok watch should never be counted as equivalent leads.

**M19** owns platform experiments and asset-level qualified funnel data. **#287** owns the shared editorial cadence and repurposing. **#286** owns source/CTA attribution and activation definitions. **M17** owns actual paid cohorts, acquisition cost/payback and retention. **M13** owns the underlying technical product truth. **Shots** owns any delegated approved creative/production; Relay does not gain a new built-in social scheduler because we want to market Relay on social channels.

### Publishing and trust checks

Before publishing any technical claim, check:
- The feature is actually implemented and running in the demonstrated environment; planned RFCs and mocked states must be identified.
- The source screenshot, client/site reference and analytics data are owned or consented for publication; secrets and private customer information are hidden.
- The change's PR/deployment/production-verification statuses are described accurately. Customer acquisition improvements aren't inferred from a merged PR.
- Content is not deceptively synthetic; reused editing assets and third-party audio have correct rights and disclosure.
- Each account, CTA, link and tracking method is genuinely available; platform API features are not assumed.
- The founder-speaking script is concrete and conversational; remove inflated claims, repetitive formulaic hooks, artificial quotas and generic startup platitudes.

**Stop condition:** if a platform creates lots of irrelevant attention but no qualified founder interest, reconsider the message or channel. If Relay cannot currently deliver the described website workflow, narrow the CTA to an accurate technical demonstration or bounded pilot. If the solo founder cannot maintain the workload, reduce output rather than manufacture content.

## The first bounded channel experiments

### Experiment A — Developer utility to first useful Relay action

Audit the real SEO Check action, its README, public checks and any current site links. Do not assume a Marketplace listing or referral flow exists. Produce one accurate example that begins with a meaningful site/PR finding and shows how the founder would investigate and approve a supported correction in Relay. If the product cannot complete the path yet, describe it as a planned capability and route to a narrowly scoped design-partner conversation instead of falsely claiming automation.

**Observe:** qualified action visitors → Relay marketing landing visits → explicit sign-in/authorized connection → reviewed relevant recommendation → paid pilot where offered. Confirm data provenance; an action installation or GitHub star is not payment.

### Experiment B — Proof-led technical founder content

Choose one permitted real example from a Playbook website or consenting customer. Record the observed problem, source and query/date, proposed and approved diff, PR result, production observation and later measurement eligibility. Build one detailed, useful post or recorded walkthrough. Repurpose it into short accurate founder-facing explanations through existing owned channels (X/LinkedIn and suitable community participation); use Shots for approved creative production if it helps. Do not create a fictional protagonist, testimonial or positive result.

**Observe:** relevant source-tagged visits, founder replies, authorized first-use decisions and meaningful conversations. Likes and impressions are secondary.

### Experiment C — Focused founder discovery

Identify actual software founders who have discussed a relevant website/search acquisition problem or are personally reachable through existing relationships. Ask about the last completed website improvement, time/effort, approval limitations and what they currently pay for. Offer a bounded, honestly described design-partner workflow with a specific owner and approved scope. Respect community rules and outreach consent; do not automate cold messages by default.

**Observe:** qualified conversations, source access decisions, actual paid offer acceptance, second-cycle interest and reasons the founder's current workflow is good enough.

## Minimal weekly operating cadence

This is a **capacity guideline**, not a mandatory content quota. One person must be able to operate it without a new marketing department.

- **Collect evidence:** review a product change, real site finding, founder objection or completed supported demo. No evidence means no fabricated story.
- **Choose one claim:** who is the reader, what exactly is verified, which founder problem matters and what we want them to do next.
- **Produce and publish:** start with one substantive technical walkthrough or useful explanation; adapt only when the source material supports alternate formats. Use existing Shots/schedulers and approved accounts.
- **Have a real conversation:** respond to useful founder questions, verify actual purchase/substitute objections and avoid mass unrequested outreach.
- **Review the funnel:** log source, qualified founder arrival, first useful action, pilot offer, payment if any, acquisition-specific staff time, what failed and which experiment to stop or repeat.

Avoid scheduling an arbitrary number of posts merely to show activity. If one source-backed demonstration creates five genuinely useful formats, publish them. If it doesn't, don't force five posts.

## Marketing measurement versus company economics

The marketing scorecard should preserve **actual count, date, cohort, attribution quality and cost**:

| Stage | Marketing measurement | Reporting rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach | Qualified founder-facing views or visits, by source/asset | Bots, unrelated readers, unknown source separately labelled |
| Interest | CTA actions, relevant conversations and explicit permission questions | An email open or content like is not a lead |
| Activation | Authorized site/project plus one genuinely reviewed, useful Relay decision | Demo content or merely created account does not qualify |
| Execution | Founder-approved source change independently verified live where eligible | PR created/merged is not enough |
| Purchase | Actual paid Grow or Protect acquisition program | Research credits/free plans excluded |
| Retention | Second meaningful review cycle and actual paid renewal | No artificial recommendations in a quiet week |
| Cost | Content time, outreach/sales time and direct expenses | Founder hours are not zero-cost |

M19 owns marketing campaigns and the conversion funnel through paid-program handoff. **M17 owns price, recurring revenue, gross/retained ARR, CAC/payback, account expansion and contribution margin** using the same sourced cohort records, including marketing acquisition time. Do not create a second analytics database or redefine payment.

## Exit criteria and explicit failure

M19's initial campaign experiment is complete when:
1. The first buyer/message is clear and published truthfully or documented as blocked by product readiness.
2. At least one identifiable distribution path has been operated and reviewed with qualified founder evidence, not just content volume.
3. Attribution from that source to a genuine first-use action is documented to the extent permitted, with refusals/missing data explicit.
4. A paid founder pilot was offered on truthful terms where appropriate; payment and nonpayment are reported honestly, not required to manufacture a win.
5. The actual acquisition-specific cost and founder time are recorded; a decision states which channel to repeat, revise or stop.

**Marketing does not need invented positive results to close an experiment.** If no paying founders convert, report it and change the offer, channel or targeting before spending more. If M13 cannot reliably perform the promised website change, narrow the headline and sell an assisted pilot honestly rather than claiming a finished autonomous growth product.

The next investment after the first experiment is decided jointly with M13 technical evidence and M17 buyer economics. The milestone is a work-and-learning boundary, not a substitute for customer demand.
