# Relay: from founder growth tool to acquisition execution company

**Strategic decision:** November 8, 2026.  
**Owner:** Playbook Media LLC.  
**Goal:** Build a company with a credible route to **$100M+ annual recurring revenue**, not a $100M forecast, valuation or near-term implementation target.  
**Entry customer:** independent software founder. **Expansion customer:** the same founder-led software company as its acquisition workload, stakes and budget grow.

This is the commercial and product expansion contract for [vision.md](../../vision.md). [The growth execution roadmap](GROWTH-EXECUTION-ROADMAP.md) remains the technical delivery companion. All new capabilities are proposals until live validation and customer authority support them.

## The uncomfortable distinction

An SEO research tool reports what may be wrong. A source editor can make a change. A small growth assistant can connect those two.

A substantially larger business must **repeatedly take responsibility for the acquisition work the customer would otherwise do**, while keeping source permissions, publication decisions and business-outcome evidence honest.

Relay's long-term paid product is a **recurring acquisition program**: the software business agrees on qualified acquisition goals, connects the specific evidence and destinations it already owns, and uses Relay to decide and complete successive bounded improvements. The program remembers prior changes and their observed results, runs on a weekly cadence and becomes more valuable as the business acquires traffic, products, target segments and revenue.

The company must graduate along four independent dimensions:

1. **Commercial responsibility:** from one SEO recommendation to an agreed recurring acquisition program covering discovery, controlled site changes, key conversion paths and selected distribution.
2. **Recurring usage:** from a one-off PR to repeat, source-backed review → approved work → verification → learning, including quiet weeks.
3. **Contract value:** from low-cost research to bounded ongoing programs for founders whose acquisition operations have material economic value.
4. **Outcome credibility:** from shipping a page to reporting qualified acquisition behavior and, when sound designs permit, estimating incremental impact.

**None of these follows automatically from the others.** A large number of cheap users without retention does not establish a large business. More integrations without permissions or useful repeat actions increase support costs. A before/after chart is not proof of a customer acquisition gain.

## The customer we land with and retain

| Customer state | Pain worth testing | Appropriate early offer | What earns expansion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pre-traffic founder** | Product exists but has little search evidence or acquisition capacity | Bounded site health, relevant market research and first-page plan; call insufficient data what it is | First observable inbound opportunity, source-controlled site and willingness to pay for repeated work |
| **Early inbound founder** | Needs to identify which pages, queries and landing-page changes matter | Supported opportunities, monthly/weekly review, limited approved improvements and honest measurements | Repeat approved work, useful acquisition events, additional work capacity |
| **Revenue-generating founder-led SaaS** | More acquisition surface and cost of mistakes, multiple intents/journeys and more marketing decisions than one founder can review manually | Recurring acquisition program, prioritized review cadence, protected high-value paths, measured conversion and more approved actions | Multi-site/product programs, response expectations, more destinations and greater review capacity |
| **Scaled founder-led software company** | Multiple products, acquisition teams, measurable pipeline and a larger opportunity cost of delayed work | Controlled multi-program offering with precise roles, event definitions, reporting and service levels | Verified business value and meaningful recurring adoption across the accounts, with an economic buyer |

Do **not** define the first paying buyer as an enterprise operations committee. The successful expansion path is that a founder keeps Relay as their company grows. Later distribution to larger businesses is a separate hypothesis, not a reason to build RBAC and platform breadth before the first paid program.

## The unit of customer value: an acquisition program

A **program** has a founder-approved site/product, target customer or intent, qualified conversion goal, review cadence, allowed actions, named owner, provider budget, data sources, explicit no-action condition and destination verification rules.

A **weekly acquisition cycle** records: which source data remained current, what changed, what was investigated, what priority was accepted/rejected, what work progressed, whether the destination was verified and when a result becomes eligible for observation. It may legitimately end with no proposed edits. An unattended cron tick with no source coverage or owner engagement is **not** a meaningful weekly product use event.

A **work item** ties one evidence-backed hypothesis to the exact action, permissions, technical postcondition and distinct commercial observation clock. GitHub branches, attempted writes, AI drafts, retries and alternative copy versions are children of one work item, not multiple customer wins.

### The weekly service contract

1. **Collect and reconcile** relevant first-party and paid-provider observations with freshness and spend status.
2. **Choose** a small set of eligible actions ranked by buyer relevance, evidence, downside, effort and change history. Founders can reject or defer.
3. **Prepare and authorize** an exact supported action, with safe refusal for unproven sources or claims.
4. **Ship and verify** the actual destination state, under the customer's release and publishing controls.
5. **Observe and adjust** the later exposure, behavior and economic outcomes when the data is eligible; preserve inconclusive/unknown evidence.
6. **Carry context forward** into the following cycle, including why an action failed, what the founder refused, and where provider or reviewer time was spent.

A program has a bounded number of sites, sources, action classes, active reviews and provider requests. Charging for unlimited agent time or promising a fixed volume of edits would reward bad behavior.

## The product grows from the existing stack

**Phase 1: existing-site organic growth.** Reuse DataForSEO, Search Console, audits, opportunity queue, GitHub editor, approvals and experiments. Complete one real deployment-verified existing-page change (M13). The original Protect path and candidate citations are useful adjuncts, not universal prerequisites.

**Phase 2: recurring acquisition cycles.** Layer a founder review cadence, history/cooldown, cost and supported repeat playbooks on top of the existing Work area (M15, M5 and M10). Do not build a second planning application.

**Phase 3: qualified acquisition evidence.** Let the founder choose a meaningful conversion definition and consented connected source. Reuse GA4 and existing read-only Stripe attribution where eligible, retain honest coverage and source limits, and separate the outcomes ledger from the code deployment ledger (M16, selected M9).

**Phase 4: higher-value commercial packages.** Sell recurring **programs**, not access to more keywords. Validate bounded workload, pricing, service costs, retention and account expansion as founder-led software businesses grow (M17 and M7). Higher plans earn their price through more repeatable work and reliability, not cosmetic enterprise permissions.

**Phase 5: selected channels and products.** When paid customers ask Relay to coordinate a second channel, prefer an explicitly permitted handoff to an existing specialized system, with published/received receipts and the right outcome source (M18). Shots makes and distributes release creative; Verify/Warmbly owns outbound contact quality and sending. Relay owns the acquisition diagnosis and records only the evidence each system is authorized to share.

**Deferred:** unlimited content farms, an ad-spend buying engine, universal marketing CRM, independently replicated email composer, unrestricted writer agents, a general product-fact graph, full Relay-owned launch kits and untrusted code execution before its safety gate.

## Where qualified acquisition gets measured

### Four different evidence clocks

| Layer | Exact question | First supported evidence | What a positive result does **not** prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Execution** | Did the approved change reach its intended destination and satisfy the specified postcondition? | Source SHA, approved diff, exact PR/merge verification, independent production snapshot | That any customer saw it or that it increased demand |
| **Exposure** | Did relevant people have more opportunities to discover this product? | Search Console query/page impressions, clicks, consented channel/session observations, sampled AI visibility | Which people later bought, or that the change caused the movement |
| **Behavior** | Did potential customers do a meaningful next step? | Founder-defined signup, qualified trial, demo, activation or other consented aggregate event | That all events are captured, qualified, deduplicated or driven by one edited page |
| **Economics** | Did the business acquire paying customers or qualified pipeline? | Authorized, correctly scoped billing/customer or CRM aggregates where source and consent permit | Incremental revenue attributable to Relay without appropriate identification |

A business value report must say **which** of these layers has evidence, and which is unknown. Never represent a merged PR as a verified deployment; never represent a successful deployment as increased acquisition; never represent first-touch attribution as causal incrementality.

### Evidence grades, not a fabricated ROI number

- **Verified technical completion:** independent, fresh proof of the original live destination postcondition.
- **Descriptive observed movement:** clear before/after source, comparable dimensions and coverage, with confounders visible; not causal.
- **Comparable observational analysis:** a carefully defined reference period/group with assumptions and contamination limitations; stronger interpretation but not randomized.
- **Controlled experiment:** only with defensible assignment, sufficient power and preserved execution design. If eligibility fails, report inconclusive.
- **Unknown/inconclusive:** absence of connected data, broken tagging, sparse sample, changed event definitions, delayed attribution, inaccessible rollout or insufficient measurement window.

The existing Search Console experiment flow documents a 28-day baseline, seven-day exclusion period and 28-day observation window, with human deployment confirmation. Maintain those safeguards where applicable, and do not claim all delivery or measurement refreshes run automatically before verifying their implementation.

### Outcome attribution and customer trust

Start with verified page/intent and aggregate goal data. Add path-to-trial or payment evidence only when the founder has a permitted, stable identity relationship and a clear data-retention rule. Avoid raw personal data joins between analytics, billing and outbound tools as the default. Show which channels or other product changes could explain a movement. A model's explanation is not statistical identification.

The company should publish one redacted case study only after a customer authorizes it and the evidence supports precisely the result claimed. Technical time saved can be measured directly; incremental acquired customers require stronger proof.

## North-star and company operating metrics

**North-star product metric to test:** *weekly paying acquisition programs with a completed evidence-backed decision cycle*. Count each authorized, paid program at most once per week when source coverage is usable and a founder or authorized reviewer made a supported decision, progressed approved work, or explicitly accepted a documented no-action result. An automated empty report alone does not count.

Preserve the previous **monthly paying workspaces with a verified acquisition-related production change** as a companion *delivery* metric. Do not increase this by incentivizing unnecessary edits.

**Economic metrics that determine whether a large company is possible:** paying customers, ARR, gross retention, net revenue retention, average contract value/ARPA by stage, expansion/contraction, gross margin **including** provider and routine human operating cost, acquisition cost and payback, and time to become productively active. Early cohorts should show absolute customers and dollars as well as percentages.

**Customer metrics:** qualified events correctly defined per buyer, observable paid conversions where permitted, time/effort saved for approved work, verified work outcomes, repeat meaningful cycles, source coverage and false recommendations. Distinguish observational movement from incremental outcomes.

### Illustrative scale math, not a forecast

`ARR = paying accounts × average monthly subscription value × 12`.

| Paying accounts | Average monthly subscription | Annualized revenue |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10,000 | $833.33 | ~$100M |
| 5,000 | $1,666.67 | ~$100M |
| 2,000 | $4,166.67 | ~$100M |
| 1,000 | $8,333.33 | ~$100M |

These scenarios reveal why a $29–$99/month research-only product needs an implausibly large acquisition engine to reach this scale. They say **nothing** about realistic reachable customers, churn, sales cost or willingness to pay. Relay cannot assume a large account base or enterprise ACV merely because the arithmetic works.

### Commercial gates

- **M13:** real supported opportunity → approved source edit → production verification; technical viability, not growth or retention proof.
- **M7/M17 early:** seek five to ten paid design partners as a *target*, with an actual contract, clear scope, source permissions and a second qualifying review cycle.
- **M15:** evidence of useful repeated work across weeks, including no-action weeks. Reject repetitive artificial work.
- **M16:** correctly scoped qualified conversion definition with reliable source coverage and accurate separation of delivery, acquisition and economic evidence.
- **M17 growth:** renewal, contribution margin and at least one real account expansion or an explicit finding that expansion has not been demonstrated. Do not publish optimistic NRR from two tiny accounts.

## Distribution economics: earning enough customers to matter

Pricing and retention cannot carry a $100M ARR company without a repeatable way for qualified software companies to discover, trust and buy Relay. Do not confuse customers' acquisition outcomes with **Relay's own customer acquisition funnel**.

The detailed contract is [Founder Acquisition & Distribution](FOUNDER-ACQUISITION-DISTRIBUTION.md), owned by [M17 issue #280](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/issues/280). Test one channel at a time: developer-first entry via the existing GitHub SEO check, a founder-led real-work case study, or a focused founder discovery cohort. Each needs source-tagged qualified arrivals, product authorization, first useful action, paid program adoption, second-cycle use, renewal and contribution economics. Do not announce any of these as an established growth channel without data.

### Distinguish product and company funnels

| Funnel | Unit | Evidence to record | What not to infer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Relay customer acquisition** | Qualified software-founder account | Channel, first need, first useful decision, permissions, pricing acceptance, payment and CAC | Free research visits or GitHub installs are revenue customers |
| **Relay customer product value** | Paid acquisition program | Reviewed work, approval, technical completion/no-action, provider and review costs | A scheduled check or generated page is useful work |
| **Relay customer's acquisition result** | Qualified prospect, trial or buyer | Consented source, event semantics, coverage, timing and attribution constraints | A technical change automatically created a paying customer |
| **Relay account expansion** | Same paying organization | Actual extra contracted recurring work, renewal/upgrade dollars, additional owner capacity and service cost | Feature usage or hypothetical willingness to pay is expansion ARR |

### CAC payback and retention arithmetic

Compute channel acquisition cost from attributable sales/marketing spend plus the human effort used to acquire and onboard the account, divided by **new paying customers**. Keep any founder time estimate explicit. Do not treat customer delivery costs as acquisition costs; they affect gross-profit contribution. Payback in months equals CAC divided by the account's recurring **monthly contribution after delivery/provider/routine support costs**. If contribution is zero or negative, payback is undefined, not zero.

For a small cohort, show **actual counts, billed dollars, paid dollars, churn, contraction, expansion, provider cost and manual minutes** alongside percentages. Report research plans, Grow, Protect and scaled programs separately. Do not extrapolate short pilot renewal into durable NRR. A multi-thousand-dollar ACV subscription is credible only if customers buy, use and renew a correspondingly valuable recurring service.

### Stage gates before distribution spending

1. **First trust:** prove one real authorized opportunity/approved source edit/live verification path (M13), with the cost of provider and founder effort observed.
2. **Conversion to paid:** qualify an actual founder buyer who agrees to specific monthly scope, permissions and work classes; collect payment rather than counting price-page interest (M7/M17).
3. **Repeat use and costs:** observe a second useful decision cycle (including justified no-action), a renewed customer or explicit nonrenewal, actual gross contribution and a measured acquisition channel (M15/M17).
4. **Expansion:** test larger pages/programs/qualified outcomes as the same company grows. An upgrade needs extra customer value and economic margin; release only the technical scope supported by previous verified work (M16/M17).
5. **Adjacent channels:** enable one narrow permitted handoff only when repeat paying customers request it and delivery receipts and data boundaries are proven (M18).

The go/no-go decision is allowed to be **stop or narrow**. If founders prefer Search Console plus a coding assistant, refuse source access, or require costly manual SEO service to realize value, adding more channels and higher-priced tiers will not solve the underlying problem.

Relay can supply approved acquisition insight or request bounded tasks with explicit project consent and can read back allowed receipts. This coordination is optional, not an excuse to build an all-purpose marketing command center inside Relay.

A future Playbook Operator may coordinate these specialized products for independent founders. Relay's economic owner remains the **acquisition program**, not other products' databases, mailboxes or publishing calendars.

## Review discipline

Before each major commitment answer:

- What specific repeating acquisition work is already consuming the founder's attention or budget?
- What proof of qualified customer demand supports this proposed channel, product tier or adapter?
- What exact operation will Relay complete, what happens without permission, and who verifies the destination?
- What data would support an observed business result, and what would still be unidentifiable?
- What will a paying customer renew or expand because this capability is reliable?
- Which provider, compute, security and human costs make the proposed price credible?

A $100M+ business requires many customers paying meaningful amounts and remaining satisfied over time. The strategy is to **earn** the larger responsibility, not to ship an enterprise-looking platform and hope the contract values follow.
