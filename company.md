**Category hypothesis:** Code-native organic acquisition and website growth execution for independent software founders  
**Strategy updated:** November 7, 2026

## The company thesis

**Relay helps independent software founders turn observable customer demand into approved website improvements and measurable acquisition learning.**

The founder's problem is not a shortage of keyword reports, generated pages or marketing dashboards. It is that deciding *which* acquisition work matters, implementing it safely in the website they own, verifying what shipped and learning from the result usually spans several tools and takes scarce time.

Relay should do more of that work without requiring a marketing department or replacing the founder's existing GitHub, analytics and publishing tools. Start with organic search, selected AI-search visibility observations, commercially important website pages and consented acquisition-conversion evidence.

**The paid job to test:** connect one credible search/acquisition opportunity to one founder-approved website change and an independently verified production result. Later click, trial and revenue movement is observed separately, without assigning causality to a single edit.

Release assurance remains useful: for some founders, protecting existing organic traffic may be a more urgent paid job than acquiring new traffic. Test **Grow** and **Protect** against actual buyer behavior rather than preselecting Protect as the company's entire north star.

See the authoritative [growth execution vision](vision.md) and [active milestone priorities](docs/maintainers/GROWTH-EXECUTION-ROADMAP.md).

## The first customer

An independent founder operating a software product, generally without dedicated SEO engineering, with authority over a GitHub-managed website and a real acquisition problem. The first supported safe-edit stack is Next.js. Research can support a founder with sparse Search Console data, but the product must report limitations and not invent traffic or conversion histories.

Initially, the founder may be all three roles: economic buyer, reviewer and operator. Organizations with marketing departments and complex approval chains are possible later segments, not the design starting point. Agency portfolio operations require their own repeatable single-site economics.

## Commercial jobs and differentiation

| Offer hypothesis | Customer trigger | Work the customer is paying to finish | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Grow** | Inbound demand exists but the founder cannot tell what site improvement to make, or cannot spare implementation time | Find commercially relevant opportunity, prepare a supported exact site edit, get approval, verify live state, track later observations | No ranking/revenue guarantee; do not manufacture a cause from before/after metrics |
| **Protect** | A valuable acquisition page breaks after deployment or the founder needs reliable technical coverage | Scoped site checks, consequential incident, narrow approved repair, verified original-rule resolution | Only promise coverage/cadence that's observed and affordable; no fictitious protection from creating a project |
| **AI-search experiment** | The founder has credible evidence that specific relevant AI answers/citations omit or misrepresent their product | Gather a repeatable observation and suggest evidence-backed on-site work within their control | An AI answer is volatile; no assured citation placement |

OpenSEO, Ahrefs, Semrush and other SEO tools offer research; coding assistants can edit source. Relay must prove the extra value of using trustworthy opportunity signals, a narrow authorized change in the *customer's repository*, independent production checks and retained decision history. A GitHub PR button or more research metrics alone are not a defensible business.

## What is already built

The MIT-licensed [OpenSEO](https://github.com/every-app/open-seo) foundation and Relay additions contain keyword and competitor research, rank tracking, backlinks, AI visibility, site audits, Search Console and GA4, evidence-backed opportunities, investigations, narrow GitHub publishing, experiments, selected conversion/revenue signals, automated jobs, auditing, credits and spending limits, tenant controls, and a weekly work view.

This is implementation evidence, **not** proof of complete growth execution on an external customer's deployment or commercial retention. Production regression baselines, incident-sourced repairs and independent original-rule verification have separate remaining Protect acceptance contracts. Some docs reflect historical decisions made before the November growth direction; preserve their requirements but do not treat every later RFC as an approved build order.

Retain MIT attribution, reviewed upstream maintenance, supported Docker/Cloudflare hosting and dual SQLite/Postgres tests. Reuse existing services before introducing any new agent execution framework or generic connector platform.

## Product and portfolio boundaries

- **Relay:** website acquisition research, priority selection, narrow source-controlled SEO/landing-page improvements, important technical-page protection, observational measurement and learning.
- **Shots:** visual production, brand assets, product screenshots, release-communication kits, social/media distribution, and downstream campaign materials. It owns the full release-readiness/launch-product motion.
- **Verify and Warmbly:** contact discovery/validation and outbound campaign execution, subject to their own product boundaries and consent requirements.
- **Playbook Operator:** potential cross-product workflow coordination for independent founders. Relay should supply secure, bounded operations rather than evolve into a general-purpose agent supervisor.

Shared source facts can be exchanged through authorized, versioned interfaces if a paid workflow requires it. Don't build two competing release-intelligence and launch systems merely because both products connect to GitHub.

## Pilot: find a repeat paid job

Test the two paid wedges among actual founders. A practical first program, with durations counted from the day it starts rather than from this document:

1. Reconstruct real recent website/acquisition decisions with qualified founders; record current tools, developer time, budget and permissions. Pursue a small group of paid/restricted pilots.
2. For Grow, demonstrate an evidence-backed existing-page opportunity → founder-approved exact diff → customer merge/deploy → verified live page → honest follow-up measurement. Run the same workflow on Playbook properties as engineering rehearsal, not as customer traction.
3. For Protect, validate scope, cadence, false-alert rate and a production-verified repair on authorized properties where owners actually recognize the need.
4. Measure paid acceptance, repeat use, renewals when due, reviewer labor, setup burden, support expense, provider/AI cost, observed acquisition data, and reasons prospects decline.
5. Choose to continue, narrow, reposition or stop based on payments and repeat completion. Do not promote unrelated RFCs because they are easy to implement.

Research-only $10/month Base Plan usage and metered credits remain a separate existing offer; they are not evidence of a viable Grow or Protect subscription. New pricing and entitlement rules require their own paid pilot and usage-cost work.

## What we count

The primary product-value metric proposed by [vision.md](vision.md) is **monthly paying founder workspaces completing at least one independently production-verified acquisition-related site change**. This measures completed work, **not acquired customers or traffic uplift**.

Pair it with actual qualified acquisition outcomes where data exists, observed rather than causally attributed; paid repeat behavior, retention, gross margin, time-to-first-approved-change, technical verification reliability, false opportunity rate, and full provider/human costs. Workspaces with insufficient data or no justified changes retain a valid no-action state and must not be forced to publish.

If the customer will not authorize source access, does not need the workflow repeatedly, or will not pay beyond generic research tools, revisit the product before expanding it.

## Execution policy

The current first-value milestone is [M13, first founder growth win](https://github.com/PlaybookMediaLLC/relay/milestone/13). Related milestones preserve existing evidence and safe editing (M1–M3), record pilots (M7), and add work history and bounded repeat improvements (M5/M10). M9 conversion instrumentation and M14 AI-search actions are selective or demand-gated expansions.

**Not an automatic roadmap:** the earlier Protect → Product Reality Engine → Sync → Launch progression was documented in the October 25–28 strategy. Its 46 source RFCs and 31 implementation issue records remain available as engineering specifications, but broad PRE/Sync and Relay-owned Launch work are on hold pending a separate founder value and paid-demand case. [RFC milestone compatibility](docs/maintainers/RFC-MILESTONE-TRACEABILITY.md) records the mapping; the [growth execution roadmap](docs/maintainers/GROWTH-EXECUTION-ROADMAP.md) decides what to do now.

Every new action preserves explicit project/tenant authorization, exact source revisions, necessary human approval, safe preview and CI constraints, independent destination checks, privacy and bounded provider cost. Agents can propose work but cannot silently publish or merge.

The question that decides the next investment is simple: **Which acquisition job is a founder already struggling to complete, will they pay Relay to finish it, and can Relay verify the result at sustainable cost?**
